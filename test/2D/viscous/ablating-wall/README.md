# Ablative wall boundary conditions (BC 503 / 504) and the SRM grain (BC 502)

Verification case for the gas-surface interaction wall boundary conditions,
against an analytic solution. No solver-level case exercised these boundary
conditions before.

## Setup

A thin gas column, one-dimensional in `y`:

```
   face 4 (j = ny)   outflow (extrapolation)
 +---------------------+
 |                     |
 |     gas column      |   faces 1, 2 : symmetry  -> the solution is 1-D
 |                     |
 +---------------------+
   face 3 (j = 1)    ablating wall, BC 503 or 504
```

The wall blows gas into the column; it vents at the top. The column is
initialised isothermal at the wall temperature and at rest, so the velocity
field has to be generated by the blowing itself.

## Why it has an analytic solution

With the column isothermal the conductive flux at the wall vanishes, and the
surface energy balance collapses to a single scalar equation:

* **melting (503)** — the wall temperature is prescribed, so the mass flux
  follows directly,
  `mdot = qrad / (dh + cp (Tw - Ti))`;
* **pyrolysis (504)** — the mass flux obeys an Arrhenius law in `Tw`, and `Tw`
  is the root of `q_pyro(Tw) = qrad`, found by bisection in `verify.py`.

With `p` and `T` uniform the density is uniform too, so continuity fixes the
blowing velocity at `v = mdot / rho` everywhere in the column. The isothermal
blown column is thus an exact steady solution, and the test checks that the
solver both reproduces it and reports the right wall quantities.

`verify.py` also checks the algebraic identities of the wall models themselves
(`mdot * heat_absorbed = q_wall`, `mdot = Arrhenius(Tw)`), which hold for
whatever state the solver reached and so test the boundary condition rather
than the convergence. Those hold to round-off.

## Running

```sh
./run.sh                      # every configuration, as CTest runs it
CONFIGS=hdpe ./run.sh         # just one

python3 build_case.py hdpe    # generate mesh / IC / BC by hand
./MOSE.sh solve
python3 verify.py
```

## Configurations

| name | BC | what it covers |
|---|---|---|
| `melting` | 503 | prescribed wall temperature, radiatively driven |
| `htpb`, `hdpe`, `pp` | 504 | the three pyrolysis models |
| `melting-top`, `hdpe-top` | 503 / 504 | the same walls on the opposite face, which exercises the other branch of the face-orientation bookkeeping |
| `melting-inert` | 503 | a surface that *cannot* ablate: no radiation and a gas colder than the wall, so the melting energy balance returns a non-positive mass flux and the boundary condition has to fall back to an impermeable isothermal wall |
| `srm`, `srm-top` | 502, mode 1 | a burning propellant grain, a wall that blows its products in at the flame temperature with the mass flux of Saint-Robert's law, on both faces |
| `srm-inflow`, `srm-inflow-top` | 502, mode 2 | the same grain as an inflow, the mass-flux inlet 404 at the total flame temperature, on both faces: from rest, which closes an inlet 404 alone |
| `default-schmidt` | 503 | the `melting` column with no `schmidt` in `input.ini`, run in `default-schmidt/`: the default, 0, is mixture-averaged multicomponent diffusion, which needs the binary diffusion table `INPUT/diffusion.dat` only with more than one species, so this single-species gas runs without it |

## Why the whole column is checked, wall cell included

An ablating face carries its complete flux — wall pressure, the momentum and
enthalpy convected in with the blown mass, and the viscous terms — and no
reflective wall flux is applied alongside it. A reflective (symmetry) treatment
imposes `u.n = 0` at a face that is deliberately permeable and returns the
impermeable-wall pressure, below the pressure of the cell since the gas moves
away from the face (by about `rho*c*u_blow` in the exact reflective Riemann
problem): negligible against `p`, but comparable to the pressure differences
that drive a low-speed blown boundary layer. With it in place the
wall-adjacent cell carried a mesh-independent ~36 % mass-flux deficit; the
continuity check covers every cell precisely because that is now gone.

## Surface reactions (BC 505)

`carbon`, `carbon-top` and `carbon-o2` run in `gsi/<config>/` on the gas `test/common/CHO-N2-viscous` (the eight
species of `GSI_Carbon_Bradley` and N2): a column of N2 with H2O and CO2 at 1800 K (endothermic reactions, `q_rad > 0`)
on both faces, and one of N2 with O2 at 1500 K (exothermic, `q_rad < 0`). `q_rad` is the heat the reactions absorb at the
initial state, so the balance starts with its root at T0. The reactions consume the oxidisers of the wall cell and the
products displace them: this column has no steady state, and `verify.py` checks the relations of the wall model at the
state the solver reached — the rates of `gsi_models.py` (an independent copy of `Lib_GSI.f90`, with the enthalpies of the
committed `thermo.dat` interpolated as FLINT does) give the reported `mdot` within 1e-11, the surface energy balance
`q(Tw) = q_rad + q_conv` holds within 1e-11, and its root in `Tw` is the reported one — plus the direction of the blowing
and a uniform pressure. The drift from the initial state is printed.

## The pyrolysing ablator (BC 506)

The `ablator` configurations run 506 on the column of `carbon`, in `gsi/<config>/`: `ablator` and `ablator-top` blow the
pyrolysis gas with `phi = 0.25` and `h_v0 = -8.44e5 J/kg`, the composition of a table that `build_case.py` writes as
`INPUT/pyrolysis.dat` (`gsi_models.write_pyrolysis_table`: the water-gas shift of 1 CO + 1 H2O + 0.5 H2 on 2 pressures and
3 temperatures, every row with the elements of that mixture; a synthetic table, not an equilibrium, so no reference
pressure enters it), and are checked as the 505 columns, the rates now
`(1 + phi) mdot_ch` with the table read at the wall pressure and temperature. `ablator-phi0` (`phi = 0`, `h_v0 = 0`, no table)
must give the field and wall files of `carbon` byte for byte (one thread); `ablator-hv0` (`phi = 0`, `h_v0 = -8.44e5 J/kg`)
must not, which shows that the comparison sees a change, and its energy balance carries `-mdot h_v0`. `ablator-nofile`,
`ablator-pyro2` and `ablator-cond` must stop at set-up: no table file, `pyro_id = 2` with one table, a table with a
condensed-carbon column. The species sources, the element balance of the blown gas and the pressure of the lookup are
checked by the unit check `GSIAblator`.
